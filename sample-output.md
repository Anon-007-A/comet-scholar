## Title: Literature Review — AI in Education (sample)

### Introduction
Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming educational practices by enabling personalized learning, automation of administrative tasks, and data-driven decisions. This review synthesizes recent findings and highlights areas for future research.

### Methodology Analysis
The sample papers include empirical studies, large-scale literature syntheses, and policy analyses. Sample sizes range from case studies (n < 50) to national datasets (n > 1,000). Methods include content analysis, controlled classroom trials, and survey-based studies.

### Key Findings by Theme
- Personalized adaptive systems show improved short-term learning outcomes (Harry, 2023; Nguyen, 2023).  
- Automation reduces teacher workload but raises ethical concerns around data privacy and equity (Schiff, 2021).  
- Engagement metrics link strongly with learning gains when AI feedback is timely and context-aware (Lee, 2022).

### Research Gaps
- There are limited **longitudinal studies** tracking the impact of AI learning tools across socio-economic groups.  
- Studies seldom explore **teacher training frameworks** for integrating AI in classrooms.  
- Metrics for **learning outcomes in AI-driven systems** are inconsistent across studies.  
- Research often overlooks **ethical implications** of adaptive learning for marginalized students.  
- Few analyses compare **multiple AI models** under identical classroom conditions.

### Future Research Questions
1. How do AI-driven personalized learning systems affect academic achievement across diverse socio-economic groups?  
2. What training frameworks best prepare educators to integrate AI tools ethically and effectively in their classrooms?  
3. Which standardized metrics can reliably compare learning outcomes in AI-supported education environments?  
4. What governance models ensure adaptive learning systems remain equitable and transparent for all students?  
5. How do multiple AI models perform when deployed concurrently in identical classroom contexts?
